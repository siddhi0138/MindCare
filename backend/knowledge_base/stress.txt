Managing Stress

Stress is the body's response to any demand or challenge. Short-term stress can improve performance and focus, but chronic stress, when the body stays in a heightened state for extended periods, is linked to fatigue, headaches, muscle tension, sleep problems, irritability, and weakened immune function over time.

Common sources include work or academic pressure, financial concerns, relationship conflict, major life changes, and simply having too much on one's plate at once.

Practical strategies for managing stress:
- Prioritization: writing down everything on your mind, then identifying just one or two things that truly need attention right now, rather than trying to tackle everything at once.
- Time-boxing tasks into short, focused intervals with breaks in between (e.g., 25 minutes of focused work, 5 minute break).
- Physical movement, even a 10-minute walk, measurably lowers cortisol and improves mood.
- Setting boundaries: saying no to additional commitments when already at capacity.
- Brief mindfulness practices, such as a 2-minute breathing pause between tasks, can interrupt the stress response before it builds up.
- Social support: talking through what's overwhelming with someone you trust often reduces its perceived size.

Chronic, unmanaged stress that affects physical health, relationships, or daily functioning over an extended period is worth discussing with a healthcare or mental health professional, who can help identify underlying causes and sustainable coping strategies.
