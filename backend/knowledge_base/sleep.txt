Sleep and Mental Health

Sleep and mental health are closely linked in both directions: poor sleep worsens anxiety, mood, and stress tolerance, and conditions like anxiety and depression frequently disrupt sleep. Most adults need 7-9 hours of sleep per night for healthy cognitive and emotional functioning.

Common sleep difficulties include trouble falling asleep (often tied to racing thoughts or anxiety), waking frequently during the night, and waking too early. Screens, caffeine late in the day, irregular schedules, and an anxious "wired but tired" state are frequent contributors.

Evidence-based sleep hygiene practices:
- Keep a consistent sleep and wake time, even on weekends, to stabilize your circadian rhythm.
- Avoid screens 1-2 hours before bed; blue light suppresses melatonin production.
- Keep the bedroom cool (around 65°F/18°C), dark, and quiet.
- Avoid caffeine after early afternoon, as its effects can last 6+ hours.
- Use the bed only for sleep, not for work or scrolling, to strengthen the mental association between bed and rest.
- If racing thoughts keep you up, try a "brain dump": writing down everything on your mind before bed so it doesn't have to be held in your head.
- Progressive muscle relaxation or a short guided body-scan meditation can help the body downshift into a restful state.

If insomnia persists for several weeks, significantly affects daytime functioning, or is tied to ongoing anxiety or depression, it is worth discussing with a healthcare provider. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a well-supported, non-medication treatment.
